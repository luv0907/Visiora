<h2>What is a Distance-Time Graph?</h2>
<p>A distance-time graph (often called an s–t graph, where <strong>s</strong> represents distance and <strong>t</strong> represents time) is a two-dimensional plot that shows how far an object has traveled over a given period of time.</p>

<p>To plot a distance-time graph accurately, physicists follow standard graphing conventions:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Time (t) on the X-axis:</strong> Time is the independent variable because it flows continuously and cannot be controlled or stopped. Time is usually measured in seconds (s) or hours (h).</li>
  <li><strong>Distance (s) on the Y-axis:</strong> Distance is the dependent variable because the distance covered depends directly on how much time has passed. Distance is measured in meters (m) or kilometers (km).</li>
  <li><strong>Choosing Scales:</strong> Pick an easy-to-read scale for both axes (for example, 1 cm = 5 seconds on the x-axis, and 1 cm = 10 meters on the y-axis) so that your graph fills at least half the graphing sheet.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Three Key Types of Distance-Time Graphs</h2>
<p>The overall shape of a distance-time graph tells you the exact nature of the object's movement at a glance.</p>

<h3>1. Distance Time Graph for Object at Rest (Stationary Body)</h3>
<p>Imagine a school bus parked at a bus stop. As seconds tick by on your stopwatch (t = 1 s, 2 s, 3 s, 4 s), the bus stays at the 20-meter mark. Its position does not change at all.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Visual Shape:</strong> A horizontal straight line parallel to the time axis (x-axis).</li>
  <li><strong>Physical Meaning:</strong> The distance value remains constant as time increases. Because the change in distance is zero (Δs = 0), the speed of the body is 0 m/s.</li>
</ul>

<h3>2. Distance Time Graph for Uniform Motion Class 9</h3>
<p>Uniform motion occurs when an object covers equal distances in equal intervals of time, no matter how small these time intervals are. For instance, a cyclist covering exactly 5 meters every second is traveling in uniform motion.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Visual Shape:</strong> A straight line inclined at a constant angle to the time axis, passing through the origin (0, 0) if the object started from the reference point.</li>
  <li><strong>Physical Meaning:</strong> The straight line proves that the rate of change of distance with respect to time is constant. This means the object is moving with a constant speed.</li>
</ul>

<h3>3. Non Uniform Motion Distance Time Graph</h3>
<p>In real life, objects rarely maintain perfectly steady speeds. A car speeds away from a red traffic light or slows down in heavy traffic. Such motion is called non-uniform motion.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Visual Shape:</strong> A curved line with changing steepness.</li>
  <li><strong>Accelerated Motion (Speeding Up):</strong> If the curve bends upwards and becomes steeper over time, the body covers larger distances in each successive second. An example is a falling ball in <a href="https://www.visiora.online/blog/what-is-free-fall-class-9-notes-formula-examples">what is free fall</a>, where gravitational pull accelerates the object toward the ground. For more on how mass does not change this falling acceleration, check out <a href="https://www.visiora.online/blog/do-heavier-objects-fall-faster">Galileo's free-fall idea</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Decelerated Motion (Slowing Down):</strong> If the curve flattens out and becomes less steep over time, the body is slowing down, covering smaller distances in each successive second until it eventually stops.</li>
</ul>

<h2>How to Calculate Speed from Distance Time Graph</h2>
<p>One of the most frequent exam questions asks: <em>What does the slope of a distance-time graph give?</em> The answer is straightforward: <strong>the slope of a distance-time graph gives the speed of the moving object</strong>.</p>

<p>To understand why, let us examine the mathematical definition of slope:</p>
<p>Slope = Vertical change (Rise) / Horizontal change (Run)</p>
<p>On an s–t graph:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Rise = Change in distance = Δs = (s₂ - s₁)</li>
  <li>Run = Change in time = Δt = (t₂ - t₁)</li>
</ul>
<p>Therefore:</p>
<p><strong>Slope = (s₂ - s₁) / (t₂ - t₁) = Distance / Time = Speed (v)</strong></p>
<p>The steeper the slope of the line, the higher the speed of the object. A gentle slope represents a slower object, and a zero slope (horizontal line) represents an object at rest.</p>

<h3>Step-by-Step Solved Calculation</h3>
<p>Let us look at a practical example of finding speed from graph coordinates.</p>
<p><strong>Problem:</strong> A toy car moves along a straight floor. Its position is plotted on a distance-time graph. Point A on the line has coordinates (t₁ = 2 s, s₁ = 10 m), and Point B has coordinates (t₂ = 6 s, s₂ = 30 m). Calculate the speed of the toy car.</p>
<ol>
  <li><strong>Identify the coordinates:</strong> Initial point (t₁ = 2 s, s₁ = 10 m); Final point (t₂ = 6 s, s₂ = 30 m).</li>
  <li><strong>Find change in distance (Δs):</strong> s₂ - s₁ = 30 m - 10 m = 20 m.</li>
  <li><strong>Find change in time (Δt):</strong> t₂ - t₁ = 6 s - 2 s = 4 s.</li>
  <li><strong>Apply the slope formula:</strong> Speed v = (s₂ - s₁) / (t₂ - t₁) = 20 m / 4 s = 5 m/s.</li>
</ol>
<p>The speed of the toy car is <strong>5 m/s</strong>.</p>

<h2>Distance-Time Graph vs. Displacement-Time Graph</h2>
<p>While students often use distance and displacement interchangeably, physics treats them differently. You can review the foundational concepts in our guide to the <a href="https://www.visiora.online/blog/difference-between-speed-and-velocity-class-9-physics">difference between speed and velocity</a>.</p>
<p>Here is how their graphs compare:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Distance-Time Graph:</strong> Distance is a scalar quantity and can never decrease with time. Therefore, an s–t graph line can stay flat or rise, but it can <em>never slope downwards</em>. Its slope always gives <strong>speed</strong> (a positive scalar).</li>
  <li><strong>Displacement-Time Graph:</strong> Displacement is a vector quantity that measures shortest distance from the start point. If an object turns around and walks back toward its starting position, displacement decreases. A displacement-time graph can slope downward, drop below the time axis into negative values, and reach zero. Its slope gives <strong>velocity</strong>.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Distance Time Graph Numericals Class 9</h2>
<p>Let us solve three exam-style graph problems from <strong>NCERT Class 9 Science — Motion</strong>.</p>

<h3>Problem 1: Interpreting a Multi-Stage Journey</h3>
<p>A runner's motion is recorded in three consecutive phases on a distance-time graph:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Phase AB (0 s to 4 s):</strong> The graph is a straight line rising from 0 m to 16 m.</li>
  <li><strong>Phase BC (4 s to 8 s):</strong> The graph is a horizontal flat line at 16 m.</li>
  <li><strong>Phase CD (8 s to 12 s):</strong> The graph is a straight line rising from 16 m to 36 m.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Questions:</strong></p>
<ol>
  <li>What is the speed during Phase AB?</li>
  <li>What is the state of motion during Phase BC?</li>
  <li>In which phase was the runner moving the fastest?</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Solution:</strong></p>
<p>1. Speed in Phase AB = (16 m - 0 m) / (4 s - 0 s) = 16 m / 4 s = <strong>4 m/s</strong>.</p>
<p>2. During Phase BC, the line is horizontal (change in distance = 0 m). The runner is <strong>at rest (speed = 0 m/s)</strong>.</p>
<p>3. Speed in Phase CD = (36 m - 16 m) / (12 s - 8 s) = 20 m / 4 s = <strong>5 m/s</strong>.<br>
Comparing the speeds (4 m/s vs 0 m/s vs 5 m/s), the runner was fastest during <strong>Phase CD</strong> because that section has the steepest slope.</p>

<h3>Problem 2: Comparing Two Vehicles on the Same Graph</h3>
<p>Two cars, A and B, start from the origin at the same time. The line for Car A makes an angle of 30 degrees with the time axis, while the line for Car B makes an angle of 60 degrees with the time axis.</p>
<p><strong>Question:</strong> Which car is traveling faster? Explain why without complex trigonometry.</p>
<p><strong>Solution:</strong><br>
On a distance-time graph, steepness indicates speed. Car B is inclined at 60 degrees, which is steeper than Car A's 30 degrees. A steeper line means Car B covers more vertical distance for the same horizontal time increment. Therefore, <strong>Car B is traveling faster than Car A</strong>.</p>

<h3>Problem 3: Calculating Total Average Speed</h3>
<p>An electric scooter travels along a straight path. From the graph, it covers 60 m in the first 10 s, stops for 5 s, and then covers another 90 m in the next 15 s.</p>
<p><strong>Question:</strong> What is the average speed of the scooter over the total journey?</p>
<p><strong>Solution:</strong><br>
Average speed = Total distance traveled / Total time taken<br>
Total distance = 60 m + 0 m + 90 m = 150 m<br>
Total time = 10 s + 5 s + 15 s = 30 s<br>
Average speed = 150 m / 30 s = <strong>5 m/s</strong>.</p>

<h2>Summary & Quick Revision Notes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>X-axis:</strong> Time (t) in seconds or hours.</li>
  <li><strong>Y-axis:</strong> Distance (s) in meters or kilometers.</li>
  <li><strong>Horizontal Line:</strong> Body is stationary / at rest (speed = 0).</li>
  <li><strong>Straight Slanted Line:</strong> Uniform motion / constant speed.</li>
  <li><strong>Curved Line:</strong> Non-uniform motion / accelerated or decelerated motion.</li>
  <li><strong>Slope Calculation:</strong> Slope = (s₂ - s₁) / (t₂ - t₁) = Speed.</li>
  <li><strong>Key Rule:</strong> A real-world distance-time graph can never slope downwards or curve backwards in time because distance and time can never decrease.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions (Class 9 Motion)</h2>

<h3>What does the slope of a distance-time graph represent?</h3>
<p>The slope of a distance-time graph represents the <strong>speed</strong> of the moving body. A steeper slope represents a greater speed, while a completely flat (zero) slope represents an object at rest.</p>

<h3>What does a horizontal straight line indicate on a distance-time graph?</h3>
<p>A horizontal straight line parallel to the time axis indicates that the object is stationary (at rest). While time continues to advance, the object's position does not change, meaning its speed is 0 m/s.</p>

<h3>How do you find speed from a distance-time graph in Class 9?</h3>
<p>Select two points on the line, (t₁, s₁) and (t₂, s₂). Calculate the difference in distance (s₂ - s₁) and divide it by the difference in time (t₂ - t₁). The resulting value gives the speed in units of meters per second (m/s) or kilometers per hour (km/h).</p>

<h3>What is the difference between a distance-time graph and a displacement-time graph?</h3>
<p>A distance-time graph can never slope downward because distance is a scalar that always accumulates. Its slope gives speed. A displacement-time graph can slope downward, cross zero, or show negative values because displacement is a vector pointing from start to finish. The slope of a displacement-time graph gives velocity.</p>
